### 3.4 How `threshold_source` relates to `band_basis_provenance_class` (section 6 asks)

**They answer different questions and neither is derived from the other, which is why both are
stored.**

* `band_basis_provenance_class` — **what kind of thing the basis is**: CODIFIED / CONVENTION /
  OWNER-CALIBRATED. An *epistemic* claim: is there a standard behind this number, a widely used
  practice, or nothing but the owner's judgement.
* `threshold_source` — **which rung of the precedence order supplied the figure this reading
  used**: `project_specific` / `formal_external_basis` / `owner_configured_default`. A
  *procedural* claim about where the platform got it.

The mapping is **not** one-to-one. A CODIFIED basis can arrive from a project document (a contract
restating a regulatory deadline: CODIFIED / `project_specific`) or from the external instrument
itself (CODIFIED / `formal_external_basis`). An OWNER-CALIBRATED boundary is normally
`owner_configured_default`, but a project quality plan stating a stricter figure is
OWNER-CALIBRATED in kind and `project_specific` in source. **Rung 4 of the precedence order — no
threshold available — has no value in this vocabulary on purpose:** a reading with no threshold
asserts no band, so it never reaches `banded()`; it goes through `band_abstained` with its reason,
which is rung 4 implemented exactly.

**§12.5 is enforced by construction.** `threshold_source` is a **required keyword** on
`models.banded()` — it raises `TypeError` if omitted and `ValueError` on a value outside the three
— and the A6 route raises the same way. Both refusals are proved in `test_run102.py`. It is a new
key on `module_results`, which is `JSONType` (`research_models.py:672`, re-verified): **no column,
no migration, so §9.6's stop-and-report does not fire.**
