## 3. CUSUM canonical definition

The two-sided tabular CUSUM:

    C+_t = max(0, C+_{t-1} + (x_t - mu0) - K)
    C-_t = max(0, C-_{t-1} + (mu0 - x_t) - K)

with K = k sigma the reference value, an alarm when either arm exceeds H = h sigma, and both
arms reset to zero. Source: Montgomery, *Introduction to Statistical Quality Control*, tabular
CUSUM; corroborated against the NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods section 6.3.2.3
and Minitab's published CUSUM methods and formulas.

**The published operating characteristic at k = 0.5 and h = 5 is ARL0 = 465.44, ARL1 = 10.38 at
a one-sigma shift and ARL1 = 4.01 at a two-sigma shift.** These figures were retrieved and
cross-checked against two independent reproductions of Montgomery's ARL table.

Production implements this recurrence exactly, at k = 0.5 sigma and h = 5 sigma, on the
project's schedule performance index history with the reference value fixed at 1.0.

## 4. CUSUM known-answer example

Constructed from the recurrence, hand-derived before production was run, with the scale
**held fixed rather than estimated** so that algorithm correctness is separated from
calibration. mu0 = 1.0, sigma = 0.10, k = 0.5, h = 5, so K = 0.05 and H = 0.50.

Observations: 1.02, 0.98, 1.01, 0.99, 1.00, 0.85, 0.86, 0.84, 0.87, 0.85, 0.86

Hand-derived C-: 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.10, 0.19, 0.30, 0.38, 0.48, 0.57.
C+ stays at zero throughout. 0.48 is below H and 0.57 is above it, so the **independently known
first crossing is the eleventh observation.**

**Production reproduces every term and the crossing index exactly.** The reflected series
(x mapped to 2 - x) produces the mirror image on C+, term for term, with the same crossing
index, so **both directions are tested and both pass.** A truncated ten-observation series does
not cross, and a series exactly at target accumulates nothing on either arm.

Six mutations of the algorithm were applied to the real source bytes and each was confirmed to
have changed the file before it was run: dropping the accumulation term, reversing the sign of
the negative arm, removing K, disabling the threshold comparison, comparing against a wrong
threshold, and removing the reset at zero. **All six break the known answer.**

## 5. CUSUM calibration fixture and the objectives declared before tuning

The production signal is the schedule performance index across reporting periods, target 1.0.
The change CUSUM is intended to detect is a **persistent level shift** in that index: a project
that has genuinely fallen behind and stays behind. Gradual drift is a secondary target. An
isolated one-period excursion is not a target, for the reason given in section 7.

**Objectives, fixed in `run15_cusum_objectives()` before any parameter search ran:**

- **O1** ARL0 at least 100 periods. A project runs 12 to 36 reporting periods, so this is well
  under one false alarm per project life.
- **O2** detection rate at least 0.90 for a persistent shift of one sigma or more.
- **O3** median detection delay at most 5 periods at one sigma.
- **O4** positive and negative detection rates within 0.10 at equal shift magnitude.
- **O5** an isolated one-period spike is out of scope by design and must not be traded against
  O1 to O3.

**O3 as declared was wrong, and it was wrong against published theory rather than against our
results.** No design at k = 0.5 can have a median delay of five periods at a one-sigma shift,
because the published ARL1 there is 10.38. **O3 was re-anchored to the published table**, to at
most 12 periods at one sigma and at most 6 at two sigma, and the re-anchoring was made from the
citation before the finalists were re-run. The original mis-declaration is left in the code and
in `run15_cusum_calibration.csv` rather than deleted, so the correction stays visible. **No
other objective was touched, and none was moved to fit an attractive result.**

The fixture is thirteen labelled families over 36-period series, labels being properties of the
seeded generator and existing before any detector runs: three stable families at low, moderate
and high noise plus alternating noise; positive and negative persistent shifts at 0.5, 1.0 and
2.0 sigma; gradual positive and negative drift; an isolated spike; a two-period excursion; a
return to baseline; and a boundary-near shift at 0.25 sigma. 400 replicates per family on the
calibration set and 400 on a **separate seed stream** for the holdout. **The holdout was scored
once, after the selection was frozen.**

## 7. Isolated-spike analysis

Four scale treatments were compared rather than one being assumed:

| Treatment | Result |
|---|---|
| whole series, as shipped | ARL0 321.1; one-sigma detection 0.94 both ways; median delay 9 |
| frozen true in-control scale | ARL0 325.8; delay 7; spike detection 0.307 against 0.065 |
| reference-window standard deviation | fails the detection objective at a window of 8; viable only at 12 periods or more, and even then reaches at best ARL0 270.8 |
| reference-window moving range, MRbar over 1.128 | fails the detection objective at 8 and 12 periods; best qualifying point ARL0 159.1 |

Run 14's finding is confirmed and its cause is now located precisely. Holding the scale at its
true in-control value raises isolated-spike detection from 0.065 to 0.307, so **the whole-series
estimate does suppress spikes further**: a spike inflates the very scale that decides whether it
is a spike. The suite asserts that inflation directly.

**But 0.307 is not detection either, and that is the point. Isolated one-period spikes are NOT a
target of CUSUM by design.** A cumulative sum is built to accumulate evidence of a *sustained*
small departure; a single-period excursion is a point outlier and is the province of a Shewhart
chart. **CUSUM was therefore not altered to catch them.** Turning it into a point-outlier
detector would corrupt the method. If the platform needs point-outlier detection, that is a
separate detector and an owner decision. The suite asserts the designed behaviour explicitly:
a persistent shift breaches, an isolated excursion of the same depth does not, **and that is
recorded as correct rather than as a defect.**

## 8. Selected CUSUM production calibration and justification

**k = 0.5 sigma; h = 5 sigma; scale estimated from the monitored series. All three are the
values already shipped. Not one production parameter moved.**

The grid winner among production-realisable designs is the shipped design, at ARL0 321.1 on
calibration and 302.3 on holdout. Rejected: k = 0.25 at any h collapses ARL0 to between 37 and
165; k = 1.0 at any h drops one-sigma detection to between 0.13 and 0.60; h = 4 gives ARL0
157.7; h = 6 pushes the one-sigma median delay to 11. The tradeoff is explicit: h = 6 buys
ARL0 422.7 at a delay of 11, h = 4 buys a delay of 7 at ARL0 157.7, and h = 5 is the interior
point and also the published design.

**The scale decision is the uncomfortable one and it is reported as such.** The whole-series
estimate is *not* the methodologically preferred estimator. Control chart practice takes the
scale from a designated in-control study, and the reference-window estimators are the correct
formulation. **They were rejected on availability, not on preference:** every one of them needs
a designated in-control window of at least twelve periods, which is more history than a typical
project has before the detector must report, and at the eight-period windows this platform can
supply they fail the detection objective outright. **The whole-series estimate is retained
because it is the only treatment that both meets the declared objectives and is realisable, and
that is a limitation of the data, not an endorsement of the estimator.**

**This is SYNTHETIC / CONTROLLED OPERATING CALIBRATION. No field empirical validation is
claimed and no project data was used at any point.**

## 10. Isolation Forest canonical definition

Liu, Ting and Zhou, *Isolation Forest*, Proceedings of the 2008 Eighth IEEE International
Conference on Data Mining, pages 413 to 422, doi:10.1109/ICDM.2008.17; extended as
*Isolation-Based Anomaly Detection*, ACM TKDD 6(1), 2012, doi:10.1145/2133360.2133363.

- **Random attribute selection** at each internal node, uniformly among the attributes.
- **Random split selection**, uniformly between the minimum and maximum of that attribute.
- **Isolation trees**, proper binary trees grown to a height limit of ceil(log2(psi)).
- **Subsampling**: each tree grown on an independent subsample of size psi without replacement.
- **Path length**: edges traversed, plus c(size) at an external node holding more than one point.
- **Unsuccessful-search adjustment**: c(n) = 2 H(n-1) - 2 (n-1) / n.
- **Ensemble aggregation**: E(h(x)) is the mean path length over the trees.
- **Score**: s(x, n) = 2 ** (-E(h(x)) / c(psi)), tending to 1 as the path shortens, to 0.5 at
  c(n), and to 0 as it lengthens. A sample in which every instance scores about 0.5 holds no
  distinct anomaly.
- **Published defaults**: t = 100 trees, psi = 256. These are defaults of the algorithm as
  published, not of any library, and both were adopted.

## 11. Implementation architecture

`server/app/simulation/isolation_forest.py`, a pure-Python implementation of the published
algorithm, wired into D1.1 in `portfolio.py`.

**Why not a library.** This repository pins a deliberately pure-Python dependency set with no
numeric extension of any kind, and deploys to a small Render service. Adding scikit-learn pulls
numpy, scipy and joblib and their compiled wheels into the production image for one module. The
dependency was judged inappropriate and **the reason is recorded rather than assumed.**

**The library was nevertheless used as an independent oracle, in the development container
only.** scikit-learn 1.9.0 was installed there and compared against our implementation on a
seeded three-dimensional fixture of 512 reference points and 180 query points:

- Spearman rank correlation of the two score vectors **0.9952**
- maximum absolute score difference **0.0214**, mean absolute difference **0.0054**
- identical ROC-AUC of 1.000
- our c(256) and scikit-learn's own path-length normaliser agree to nine significant figures at
  **10.244771**

**scikit-learn is not in `server/requirements.txt` and is imported by no committed file.**

Determinism: a fixed seed constant, so a given portfolio returns the same score every time. The
randomisation is real, and a different seed grows a different forest. The four features are cost
index, schedule index, document risk and percent complete; no standardisation is applied,
because isolation forest splits on each attribute's own observed range and is scale free per
attribute.

## 12. Training, calibration and holdout separation

- **Training and reference population**: 300 seeded normal projects.
- **Calibration split**: 106 labelled cases across nine families, used **only** to select the
  threshold.
- **Holdout split**: 106 labelled cases from a separate draw, scored **once** at the frozen
  threshold.

Leakage guards: labels exist before any score is computed; no holdout case is in the reference
population; and **in production the forest is grown on the other projects and never on the
project being scored.** The retired detector formed its centroid, its spread and its threshold
from a population that included the scored project, so a project partly set its own normal.
That is fixed.

## 13. Canonical behaviour tests

`server/tools/test_run15_isolation_forest.py`, 81 checks. **Not one of them is an AUC.**

The normaliser is checked against the closed form computed with an **exact harmonic sum**, which
is not how production computes it, so the check is independent: the published ln-plus-gamma
estimate converges monotonically on the exact form, agrees to three decimals by n = 256, and its
known shortfall at small subsamples is bounded and recorded rather than hidden.

A hundred distinct trees exist; the height limit is exactly ceil(log2(psi)); internal split nodes
exist; both attributes are chosen as split attributes somewhere in the ensemble; more than fifty
distinct split values occur, so splits really are randomised; and every split value lies inside
its attribute's observed range. Path lengths are produced per tree and vary across the ensemble,
which a deterministic rule could not give. **An obvious outlier isolates in fewer splits than a
dense point, and a more extreme outlier faster still.** The score equals two to the power of
minus the mean path over c(psi) exactly, and a point whose mean path equals c(psi) scores one
half.

A directly understandable small example is included alongside the larger fixtures: nine points
packed into the unit interval and a tenth at 100. The distant point's mean path is shorter than
every packed point's and it scores as the most anomalous of the ten.

A fixed seed reproduces the score exactly; a different seed does not; permuting the reference
population leaves the score within 0.05. **The algorithm file contains no distance, centroid,
standardised-deviation or Euclidean arithmetic anywhere, so no fallback can be silently used.**

**Six mutations** were applied to real source bytes, each confirmed to have altered the file:
collapsing the ensemble to a constant path length, removing the unsuccessful-search adjustment,
inverting the score exponent, sending every traversal down one branch, breaking the normalising
constant, and stopping the trees splitting. **All six change the published quantities, and the
proof requires that either the isolation ordering breaks or the scores themselves move**, so a
mutation that happens to preserve an ordering is still caught.

## 15. Threshold calibration

**Predeclared objective, fixed before any threshold was scored:** the smallest threshold whose
specificity on the calibration split is at least 0.95, that is at most one false positive in
twenty. A portfolio anomaly flag sends a project to human review, and one review in twenty is a
load a portfolio manager can carry. **The retired detector's 0.720 was not used to set this
objective and the retired threshold was not carried over in any form.**

**Selected threshold 0.576**, on the calibration split, then frozen, then evaluated once on the
holdout. Calibration metrics at that threshold: precision 0.9412, recall 0.5714, specificity
0.9600, false-positive rate 0.0400. It is stable across reference population sizes: 0.580 at 10
projects, 0.576 at 20, 0.576 at 40, 0.555 at 80, 0.576 at 300.

**Bands.** Green at a score at or below 0.5, **on the authority of the paper**, which states a
score near one half means the sample holds no distinct anomaly. Red at the calibrated 0.576. One
interior boundary two thirds of the way between, **stated as a convention of this platform and
not as a result from the paper.** Two of the three boundaries are now sourced, where none was
before.

**This is controlled synthetic calibration. It is not field empirical validation.**

Limitations: with only ten other projects to grow trees on, holdout ROC-AUC falls to 0.677; at
twenty it is 0.826; at eighty 0.911. **An isolation forest needs a population and this platform
will frequently not have one.** Absent inputs are still replaced by fixed stand-in values in the
vector builder, unchanged this run and outside the authorised change.

## 16. Legacy standardised-distance proxy disposition in code

**The standardised-distance score is no longer computed, named or reported under the isolation
forest identity.** The threshold expression that added a distance to a sum of raw per-axis
standard deviations is gone from the module, and a suite asserts its absence by source text.

The quantity survives at exactly one line, renamed `relative_distance`, **solely because D1.5
Anomaly Score composes it with the percentile rank and D1.5 is a separately registered module
this run was not authorised to change.** D1.5's output is unchanged. It is not reachable under
the isolation forest identity: the name is not on it and nothing in the D1.1 block reads it.
**It is not a second active anomaly implementation under one identity, and it is not counted as
a second active module.**

The browser never computed this arithmetic; it posts to the server and merges the result, so
there is nothing client-side to resurrect. **But the client's method descriptions still called
the module a distance proxy**, which would have had the platform describe an algorithm the
server no longer runs. Those were corrected in `assets/js/knowledge.js` and
`assets/js/ds_defensibility_data.js`.

One further behaviour change follows from the method change and is reported prominently:
**D1.1 now abstains by absence when fewer than two other projects carry signal data**, matching
the contract D1.3 already followed. A distance from a centroid is defined when the population is
one point; a forest is not.

**Independently computed expected results, all verified inside the harness before production was
touched:** the cost relationship fits an intercept of 100 and a slope of 5 and predicts 400; the

the contradiction degree is zero on the diagonal, symmetric, and taken against a designated
dominant value; the Born probabilities are 0.36 and 0.64 and sum to exactly one; the nondominated
set of the four alternatives is A, B and C with D dominated by B and the answer invariant under
reordering; the Wyndor Glass optimum is **36 at x = 2, y = 6**, reproduced here by independent
vertex enumeration; and the decision rule flips under a minus one-tenth schedule perturbation but
not under a plus one-tenth cost perturbation, with a zero perturbation as control.

**What each module actually computes, and the decisive evidence.**

- **A3.8** computes the ratio of a cost-index forecast to a remaining-work forecast, an algebraic
  identity in the two. **The result is invariant under every cost driver quantity supplied**,
  which is the one property a parametric estimate must not have. No driver set, relationship or
  fitted coefficient exists anywhere in the repository.
- **B2.20** genuinely keys on a three-attribute tuple over disjoint value sets, and all
  twenty-seven tuples are reachable. But **six of the twenty-seven fall through to a silent
  default of 0.35**, so the mapping is not total on the product. *Run 14 recorded two; the
  difference is that this run exhausts the product rather than sampling it, which is exactly the
  failure mode the discipline notes warn about.* The image is a scalar, not a subset, and there
  is no universe of objects for it to be a subset of.
- **B2.7** carries appurtenance and contradiction figures and aggregates using both, so part of
  the structure is real. But **no dominant attribute value is designated anywhere**, so the
  contradiction degree is not defined relative to anything, and the figure attaches to a single
  band rather than to a pair of attribute values, so the diagonal property cannot even be posed
  against it. The degrees are literals.
- **B2.9** takes square roots of averaged literal classical probabilities. **The squared
  amplitudes do not sum to one**, so there is no normalised state. No state vector, projector or
  measurement appears. **The phase angle takes only the four values a tally of three indicators
  can produce.** This is weighted scoring wearing the vocabulary of the method, and it is
  recorded as an implementation mismatch and not as a literature problem.
- **B4.1** returns the equally weighted mean of three normalised indicators for one project. No
  alternative, decision variable or feasible region appears, the weights are unstated, and
  **supplying a set of alternatives changes nothing** because there is no argument for one.
- **B4.2** returns the cost index a project would need to finish inside its remaining budget:
  one ratio with a threshold on it. **Supplying an objective and constraints changes nothing.**
  An unbounded case cannot be represented at all.
- **B4.5** reports each input's share of a scaled total distance from fixed reference values. A
  real input change does move the shares, **but no decision is recomputed at any point** and
  there is no argument for a perturbation size.
- **B4.6** performs three threshold tests on one project, labelled with the vocabulary of
  dominance. **Supplying a set of alternatives including one that strictly dominates the project
  changes nothing at all**, because no alternative is ever seen.

**Root-cause classification, one primary per module**, with secondary causes recorded in the CSV:
four **IMPLEMENTATION_DEFECT** (B2.7, B2.9, B2.20, B4.5) and four
