# Training Simulation — US Contract Regimes

Three US contract forms, sourced. The teaching point is that the deadline which matters is the
one in *this* contract, not the one remembered from the last job. The brief for each run names
the jurisdiction and the contract form, and the periods follow from it.

**Copyright note:** A201 and ConsensusDocs are licensed documents. Cite clause numbers and state
the periods. Do not reproduce clause text. FAR and the Contract Disputes Act are public law and
may be quoted freely.

## The comparison that teaches

| Event | AIA A201-2017 | ConsensusDocs 200 | Federal (FAR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notice of claim, cost or time | **21 days** (§15.1.3.1) | **14 days**, then documentation within **21 days** after the notice (§8.4) | Changes: no fixed notice period, but **no adjustment for costs incurred more than 20 days before** written notice (FAR 52.243-4(d)) |
| Differing site conditions | **14 days** from first observance (§3.7.4) | **Stop affected work** and give prompt written notice; no fixed day count (§3.16.2) | **Promptly, and before the conditions are disturbed** (FAR 52.236-2(a)) |
| Who decides first | Initial Decision Maker, pre-substantial-completion only (§15.2.1) | Owner, then the contract's dispute mitigation procedure | **Contracting Officer**, final decision appealable |
| Losing the right to escalate | **60 days** from an IDM decision demand or binding dispute resolution is waived (§15.2) | Per the elected dispute resolution provision | CO decision becomes final if not appealed |
| Certification | None | None | Claims **over $100,000 must be certified** (FAR 52.233-1, 41 USC 7103) |

### The four traps worth building a scenario around

1. **A201's 14-day differing-site-conditions period.** It was 21 days in the 2007 edition and was
   shortened in 2017. Experienced people get this wrong from memory, and the claim is time-barred
   before they check.
2. **ConsensusDocs is a two-step clock.** Notice within 14 days, then supporting documentation
   within 21 days after that notice. Giving notice and then going quiet loses it.
3. **The federal 20-day lookback is not a notice deadline, it is a cost cutoff.** A contractor who
   works a change for six weeks and then notices recovers only the last 20 days. Nothing is
   time-barred; the money is simply gone.
4. **The federal $100,000 certification threshold is jurisdictional.** An uncertified claim over
   the threshold is not a claim at all, and the CO has no duty to decide it. A claim that grows past
   $100,000 during preparation and is submitted on the old form is the classic version.

### Two more federal mechanics worth modelling

- **Do not disturb the condition.** FAR 52.236-2 requires notice before the condition is disturbed.
  A contractor who excavates through it and reports afterwards has prejudiced the Government's
  ability to verify, and entitlement is arguable. This is a decision a PM makes in the field in
  minutes.
- **The 60-day decision clock.** For claims of $100,000 or less the CO must decide within 60 days
  of a written request. Over $100,000, the CO must within 60 days either decide or state when a
  decision will issue. Failure is a deemed denial the contractor can appeal (FAR 33.211, 41 USC
  7103).

### Service, not just sending

A201 permits most notices by email, but **Article 15 claims must go by certified or registered
mail or a courier with proof of delivery.** A trainee who "sent it" may not have served it. Clean
discrete failure with no ambiguity.

## Safety and stoppage layer

Applies across all three regimes; the strictest common US regime is the NYC DOB one.

- On issue of a stop work order, **all work stops immediately.** Only safety work continues:
  temporary shoring, fencing, securing open excavations.
- Continuing work under an SWO escalates to enforcement including criminal prosecution.
- Lifting requires a **Certificate of Correction (AEU2)**, plus whatever the cause demands: updated
  plans, affidavits, inspection reports, permit amendments. **The cause determines the paperwork,
  which is why the response matters more than the incident.**
- Rescission must be confirmed in writing; online status lags 24 to 48 hours.
- Local Law 196 training obligations sit on the **permit holder**, so a supervision gap is the
  permit holder's exposure, not only the subcontractor's.

**Duration of a stoppage is not set by regulation.** It follows from cause, paperwork and
re-inspection, so it belongs in the elicited layer below.

## Elicited layer — no authority, yours to set

Mark these as designed figures in the training material. They are what make the loop teach.

- Days lost to a stoppage, strong response versus weak.
- Productivity loss on restart, and how many periods it persists.
- Acceleration cost multiplier over the underlying work.
- Probability that a near miss becomes an SWO, and how acceleration changes it.
- What credibility with the owner is worth in a later dispute.
- Contingency drawn when a change is absorbed rather than pursued.

## The chain

A decision spends float. Float spent moves the forecast completion date. Once float is gone,
further consumption moves the contract date and puts liquidated damages in play. Recovery means
acceleration, which costs disproportionately and raises exposure. A discrete near miss can become
a stop work order at any point, and its cost depends entirely on how much float remains, which is
a function of every earlier decision.

Three tensions:

1. **Escalate** protects entitlement, spends float.
2. **Absorb** protects the relationship, spends contingency.
3. **Defer** protects both, and closes the notice window.

## Verify before relying on it

FAR and CDA figures above are from primary sources (acquisition.gov, eCFR, 41 USC). A201 and
ConsensusDocs periods are from secondary summaries by construction law firms, not the licensed
documents. Confirm both against the licensed forms before a colleague relies on them. Contract
periods are also routinely amended in negotiation, so a real project may not match its own form.
