actually happens. It left it level, and the reason is a property of this fixture and not of the
change:

* **A4.8 was already banding Yellow on this fixture.** Its two firms are rated Very Good and
  Satisfactory with no adverse trade records, so the normalised posture was never one the hold
  caught, and there was no lift of two or more bands.

  never on that path.

So on this fixture no module was held before, and none could start banding by the hold coming
off. **No movement was manufactured.** The change is measured instead on fixtures that DID hold:

## 4. GOAL 3 — AN OPEN CRITICAL NONCONFORMANCE MAKES THE SCHEDULE UNRELIABLE

### 4.1 Which records qualify — and I chose, and say so

The same `_KIND_NCR` population and the same `_QUALITY_OVERRIDE` critical vocabulary the quality
override already reads: **open critical structural, life-safety, code, hold-point or
commissioning-blocking nonconformance**. The owner said "an issue in quality" and named no wider
set; the tree settles nothing wider. The order says to use the critical set and say so where the
tree does not settle it. **This run chose the critical set and says so.** Reading a wider set
would make schedule reliability adverse on evidence the owner's own quality override does not
treat as critical — inventing a threshold.

Proved to discriminate: a **minor**, **observation** or **major** nonconformance fires neither
override. Three checks. And a **closed** critical one fires neither — goal 2 governs goal 3.

### 4.2 How adverse — it fires the schedule override, Red outright

I reasoned it rather than defaulting to it. "Move it by a band" requires a displacement rule —
from which band, by how much, floored where — and **every one of those is a threshold the owner
has not given**. The override is stated absolutely and needs none. So an open critical
nonconformance fires the schedule-reliability override exactly as it fires the quality one, and
the honest answer the order anticipated is the one the tree permits.

### 4.3 Whether it double-counts, and what it does to the weighted severity — **the briefing's arithmetic does not arise**

The briefing asked me to check 0.40×3 + 0.25×3 = **1.95** of weighted severity. The arithmetic
is right and **the mechanism is wrong**, in a way worth stating plainly:

**Overrides in this engine apply AFTER the weighted calculation, as a worst-of. They contribute
NOTHING to the weighted severity.** `firm_posture` computes
`weighted = Σ WEIGHTS[f] × BAND_SEVERITY[f.band]` from the four **ladder** bands, then
`posture = worse_of(weighted_band, override_band)`. An override never edits a factor's band.

Measured, on a firm whose four ladders are all Green:

| | schedule | quality | safety | commercial | weighted severity | weighted band | overrides fired | **published** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | Green | Green | Green | Green | **0.00** | Green | — | **Green** |
| + one open critical NCR | Green | Green | Green | Green | **0.00** | Green | schedule_reliability, quality_execution | **Red** |

**So what one open critical nonconformance does to a firm's weighted severity is: nothing. It
changes the published posture from Green to Red entirely through the override arm.**

And the counterfactual is worth the owner's eye: **if** it had been built the way the briefing
predicted — the override driving both factor bands to Red before weighting — the weighted
severity would be 0.40×3 + 0.25×3 + 0.20×0 + 0.15×0 = **1.95, which bands AMBER** (the Red cut is
2.00). The mechanism the briefing imagined is both mechanically absent and **weaker** than the
one that is there. The override is the stronger reading as well as the only one that invents
nothing.

**The double-count is real and is the owner's stated intent.** The same record fires both
overrides and both are reported: `overrides_fired` lists two, `override_detail` names the record
under each, and the schedule factor's own hit says *"an open critical nonconformance attributed
to this firm — a defect must be rectified and rectification takes time"*. Because both arms
resolve to the same Red, the double-count does not compound: one open critical nonconformance
and ten produce the same published Red. It is visible, not hidden.

### 4.4 It does not touch the Schedule category, and no date is moved

Critical Path Analysis, Milestone Trend and the rest are unchanged — the census rows for A2.1,
A2.7, A2.8, A2.9 and A2.12 are identical before and after. Nothing here computes how long a
rectification takes and no forecast completion date is written. Asserted in `drive_run121` §3.2.

**Proved able to fail** in §5.6 (`_QUALITY_OVERRIDE` emptied → the schedule override stops firing
→ FAILS → restored) and §5.7 (`_KIND_NCR` emptied → same → restored). Two separate faults,
because the check reads two separate things.
