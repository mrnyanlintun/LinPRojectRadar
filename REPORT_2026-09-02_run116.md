## The module that did not band beside the others — and the cause

The order asked me to chase exactly this. There was one, and it was **A2.7**.

The maximal project's `schedule_update` printed a full flattened schedule export in
`schedule_network_json` — activity ids, durations, three-point durations, predecessor logic,
calendars, a contractual milestone M900 with its baseline finish and its current finish. A2.1
and A2.12 both read it and both banded. **A2.7 abstained anyway — at 2 periods, at 3, and still
at 7.**

The cause, found in production code and then confirmed by measurement:

1. `documents.py` writes the `ScheduleActivity` store from **`ex.get("milestones_json")` and
   from nothing else**. `schedule_network_json` is a different table, read by a different path,
   and it never reaches the schedule store.
2. `_milestone_forecast_history` parses each milestone's baseline finish and forecast with
   `date.fromisoformat`. A **day number** — "Baseline finish: 140", which is what a network
   export prints — yields `None`, the milestone contributes no row, and the structure is absent.

Adding `milestones_json` with **ISO dates** ("Baseline finish": "2027-06-30", "Forecast
finish": "2027-07-06") moved A2.7 from ABSTAIN to BAND at 2 periods, and to Yellow at 8. Nothing
else changed.

**This corrects a premise in my briefing.** "A2.7 needs ≥2 periods of schedule tables" is true
but incomplete, and the incompleteness is the part that costs you a run: two periods of the
*wrong* schedule table produce nothing. It needs ≥2 periods of `milestones_json`, carrying
calendar dates. A project that prints a perfectly good network export and no milestone table
gets A2.1 and A2.12 and does not get A2.7.

This is category **(1)** — the document did not state it — and it was invisible from the
abstention sentence alone, which says "awaiting a milestone forecast history" and does not say
which of two schedule tables carries it.

## Stored, and stops short — category (2)

Measured by ablation: each document type removed from the maximal project in turn, the project
rebuilt and recomputed, and both the module states and the **non-null** signal-input values
compared against the intact project.

Three types put real values into the signal inputs that **no module in service reads**:

- **`historical_data`** — contributes `analogEstimate`, `externalCostIndex`, `analogousBac`,

  and **not one module changes state.** The analogous estimate and the external cost index are
  extracted, assembled and stored, and stop there.
- **`quality_audit_report`** — contributes `qualityAuditScore` (96), `totalFindings`,
  `criticalFindings`. All three disappear on removal; no module changes state. A6.1 reads the

  never looks at the field report's weather day count.**

These are the clearest category-(2) cases the platform has: the value is in the evidence store
and nothing joins it.

## How many document types the maximal project needed, and which added nothing

It needed **17 of the 27** to reach 30. Ten could be removed with **no module changing state**,
but for two different reasons, and the difference matters:

**Redundant — another document states the same thing (4 types).** Removing these loses no signal
value at all; the platform simply re-sources it.
- `pay_application` — `ac` and `actualPctComplete` re-source to `monthly_report`.
- `monthly_report` — loses nothing; `pay_application` and `schedule_of_values` cover it.
- `schedule_of_values` — `ev` re-sources to `pay_application`. **Note it wins precedence for `ev` when present.**
- `contract_value` — `baselineContractSum` re-sources to **`change_order`**.

**Contributes nothing any module reads (6 types).**
- `historical_data`, `quality_audit_report`, `field_report` — the category-(2) cases above.
- `rfa_log` — loses all six `rfa*` values. A4.3 is unaffected because the submittal register
  outranks it. **On a project with a submittal register, the RFA log is dead weight; on one
  without, it is the reason A4.3 computes without banding.**
- `correspondence_notice`, `commissioning_report` — contribute only `document_risk_score`,
  which several other types also supply. Nothing was lost and nothing changed.

**The bac finding, re-verified at this head.** On the maximal project `bac = 4,050,000`, and
its recorded source is **`change_order`, not `contract_value`**. The contract states
$4,000,000; the change order's `baseline_contract_sum` plus its net approved changes re-bases
the budget at tier 0. Every downstream figure — A1.7's TCPI, A1.8's VAC, A1.11's approved
budget, A3.6's base cost of $4,050,000 — is computed against the change order's number.
Run 112 said this happens silently. It still does, and on a maximal project it is the number
that governs six modules.

## Premises checked against the tree

- `registry.service_index()` returns **31**; `extraction_fields.DOC_TYPES` holds **27**.
- HEAD `4ad3f19`, tree clean, migration head `0033_recognition_matches`.
- `SIMULATION_VERSION = "sim-2026.09-v59"` — the briefing's correction was right, and I left it alone.
- The census fixture at this head: **17 | 1 | 13 | 0**, with A4.8 Yellow.
- ARIMA needs **8** periods and CUSUM **2** — established by refusal, not citation: A1.5 refuses at 7 and bands at 8; A1.2 refuses at 1 and bands at 2.
- A1.11 needs an approval-status column, all-or-nothing. A4.7 needs `disputes_json`. A3.6 needs real .docx bytes. C1.5 abstains on a document-only project.
- There is **no `MIN_PERIODS` constant.** The minima are not greppable and had to be derived from the runner's refusals.
- The `python /path/outside/repo/script.py` quirk is real — it returned HTTP 500 with `LookupError: Backend 'asyncio' is not available` on the first attempt. Worked around with `runpy.run_path` from `server/`, which keeps the script in the scratchpad and out of the repository.

**False or incomplete:**
1. **"A2.7 needs ≥2 periods of schedule tables."** Incomplete, and the missing half is decisive. It needs ≥2 periods of **`milestones_json` carrying ISO dates**. `schedule_network_json` — which satisfies A2.1 and A2.12 — does not feed the schedule store at all, and a day-number baseline finish parses to nothing. This cost me the run's one real chase and is the single most useful correction here.
2. **My own measurement premise, caught mid-run.** I first compared ablations by which signal-input **keys** were present. That is wrong: the platform writes the key with a **`None`** value when no document supplied it. `rfaTotal` was "present" on a project with no RFA log. Every ablation conclusion above is recomputed on **non-null values and recorded `sources`**, not key presence. Had I not checked, I would have reported that removing the RFA log loses nothing, when it loses all six of its values.
3. **B1.1's minimum.** Its census sentence names two missing inputs — the cost forecast and the performance trend — which reads as though it waits for ARIMA's eight periods. It does not: it bands at **2**, on A1.2 alone.

## What I could not establish

- **I did not run the PDF counter-test for the risk register.** A3.6 bands off real .docx bytes, which I verified. I did not independently re-confirm this run that the same register printed as a PDF yields nothing; I am relying on the earlier finding for the negative half.
- **The ablation was run at 2 periods, not 8.** 27 rebuilds at 8 periods was beyond this run's budget. A document type that matters *only* to A1.5 — which needs 8 periods — would therefore not show up as mattering. A1.5's inputs are the CPI history built from `pay_application` / `monthly_report` / `schedule_of_values`, all three of which are in the redundant group, so I believe the risk is small; I have not proved it.
- **`document_risk_score` has no measured consumer here.** Several types supply it and removing any one changed nothing, but I did not remove all of them at once, so I cannot say whether it is category (2) or merely well-covered.
- **The maximal project is one project with one set of numbers.** It proves the 30 modules *can* band together and names what each needed. It does not establish that they band on every project shape — in particular, A4.8 was authored with non-adverse ratings, because an adverse rating is deliberately HELD for Project Manager review and asserts no band. A maximal project with a Marginal subcontractor would report 29, correctly.
- **Project status "Amber" is one reading of one project.** It is not a claim about how the platform bands in general.

## Bottom line

Every module in the registry except **C1.5** can be brought into band by documents alone,
through the real upload and compute routes, with nothing supplied. It takes **17 of the 27
document types** and **8 reporting periods**. C1.5 cannot, because it needs a governed
information package definition that no document type carries — category (3), and the maximal
project is the strongest evidence for that anyone has produced.

The one module that banded on its own fixture but not beside the others was **A2.7**, and the
cause was not a period count or a defect: the platform reads its milestone history from
`milestones_json` and from no other table, and it needs calendar dates, not day numbers.

No fix is proposed and none was started.
